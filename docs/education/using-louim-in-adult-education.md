# Using LOUIM in Adult Education

Adult learners in digital-literacy or return-to-work programmes often arrive
with more real-world context than a primary student, and less patience for
being handled. A simplified interface has to read as helpful, not
condescending. Here are a few notes specific to this setting, on top of the
general [teacher guide](../teacher-guide.md).

## Say what's hidden and why it's reversible

Because LOUIM's changes are invisible unless you know to look, a missing
menu just looks like it was never there, it's worth being explicit with
adult learners. Tell them what's hidden and why: "we've hidden Tools and
Styles for this session, and everything is one click away in Restore Full
Menus whenever you want it." Framing it as decluttering rather than
restriction matters more for adults who may already use software elsewhere
and don't want to feel deskilled.

## Faster progression than primary contexts usually need

Adult learners typically move through level-1, level-2, and full faster
than younger learners do, sometimes within a single multi-hour session
rather than across weeks. Since applying is instant and non-cumulative, it's
practical to let a fast learner jump straight to `writer-full` mid-session
rather than holding the whole group to one pace.

## Task-oriented custom templates

Adult and vocational programmes are often built around concrete outcomes,
writing a CV, formatting a formal letter, building a simple budget in Calc,
rather than a general skills ladder. Save Current Layout as Template, after
setting up exactly the menus and toolbars a specific task needs, produces a
task-scoped template that's often more useful here than the generic bundled
levels. A CV-writing session, for instance, might keep Format and Insert but
drop Table and Tools. Name templates by task, `writer-cv-workshop.louim`
rather than by level, since the organizing principle for this audience is
usually what we're doing today, not how much of the interface this learner
can handle.

## Self-service once trust is established

Unlike a primary classroom, adult learners can often be shown Configure
Menus and Restore Full Menus directly and trusted to adjust their own
interface as they gain confidence. Treating LOUIM as a tool the learner
eventually operates themselves, rather than only something a teacher applies
to them, fits the adult-education context better than it does in younger
settings.
